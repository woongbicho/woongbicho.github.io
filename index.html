About me

I am a polymer materials scientist and postdoctoral scholar in Mechanical Engineering at Stanford University, working in Prof. Ruike Renee Zhao’s Soft Intelligent Materials Laboratory. My research establishes the processing–structure–properties–performance (PSPP) relationships of polymer composites: how processing conditions set the internal structure of a composite, how that structure governs its mechanical, electrical, and optical properties, and how those properties translate into application-level performance. I apply this full-cycle approach across three directions:

  1. Programmable Devices: Developing liquid crystalline polymers (LCEs and LCNs) and their composites that respond to external stimuli for on-demand shape and function tuning, enabling soft robots and actuators, shape-reconfigurable electronics, and tunable acoustic metamaterials.
  2. Value Addition of Waste Sulfur: Processing inverse-vulcanized sulfur-rich polymers and their composites into high-performance yet sustainable infrared optics (windows, lenses, and polarizers) and triboelectric nanogenerators (TENGs).
  3. Bio-Derived Polymer Composites: Compounding conventional polymers with bio-derived fillers such as cellulose and lignin to build sustainable composites.

By tracing each link from processing to performance, I aim to design polymer composites whose shape, optical, and energy functions are programmed from the processing stage onward. Feel free to reach out about collaboration or research.
