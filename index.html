About me

I am a polymer materials scientist and postdoctoral scholar in the Department of Mechanical Engineering at Stanford University, working in Prof. Ruike Renee Zhao’s Soft Intelligent Materials Laboratory. My research sits at the boundary of soft robotics, polymer chemistry, sustainable materials, and device engineering.

During my Ph.D. at Hanyang University, I developed shape-reconfigurable liquid crystalline polymer systems, sulfur-rich optical and energy materials, and bio-derived composite processing strategies. These projects share a central idea: materials become more powerful when their chemistry, architecture, and processing history are designed together. At Stanford, I study electromagnetic-driven soft robots, active metamaterials, and polymer assembly mechanisms that translate external fields into useful motion and adaptive function.

Feel free to reach out about collaboration or research.

Research

Covers: Adv. Mater. Technol. 2020; Adv. Mater. 2025.

Shape-reconfigurable devices and active metamaterials

Liquid crystalline polymers, covalent adaptable networks, stiffness gradients, and magnetic/light-responsive composites that encode motion, snap-through, locomotion, and adaptive surface function directly into material architecture.

Waste-sulfur valorization for optics and energy

Sulfur-rich polymers that transform petroleum-refining byproducts into high-index infrared optics, MWIR polarizers, 4D-printable networks, and PFAS-free triboelectric generators with MXene-segregated charge pathways.
